Video frame interpolation aims to synthesise intermediate frames between temporally adjacent boundary frames. Recent advances in diffusion models have improved the perceptual quality of the generated intermediate frames. But, under fast motion, occlusion, motion blur, and complex dynamic textures, it still remains a challenging task. 
In this paper, we present the WaveDiff model, which operates on a latent diffusion backbone, to improve the perceptual quality of the generated intermediate frames by leveraging "events" that capture motion cues. WaveDiff uses a wavelet-based event encoder to capture motion cues from the sparse event representation, along with convolutional neural network encoders to encode the bounding frames. The model performs attention-based latent event fusion to merge the appearance and semantic context from boundary frames with the motion cues from events in the latent space of the diffusion backbone. The resulting latent representation, once decoded, yields intermediate frames with improved perceptual quality. Evaluations on three popular datasets for video frame interpolation report consistent improvements over the state-of-the-art when assessed using perceptual metrics such as LPIPS, FloLPIPS, and FID. In video sequences with abrupt or fast movement, the proposed model generates the sharpest, most perceptually accurate frames, with precise foreground shapes.
